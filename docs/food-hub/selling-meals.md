# **Selling Food Hub Meals Online**

This guide is for Food Hub staff who want to sell meals online, so students can pay before they collect.

Meals are set up as **events with tickets**, not as ordinary shop products. That sounds odd at first, but it is the right fit. An event already has a date, a time, a place and a number of spaces available, and a meal service is exactly that shape. One Wednesday lunch at Rozelle with forty meals is one event with forty tickets.

> **New to this?** If you cannot see a **Food Hub** store dashboard when you log in, your access has not been set up yet. Ask a site administrator, and point them at [Setting Up Food Hub People](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/food-hub/selling-food-hub-meals-online/#setting-up-food-hub-people).



## **Why events and not products**



Using events gives you four things you would otherwise have to work around:

- **A time.** Wednesday from 12pm, rather than something on sale all week.
- **A place.** Not just which campus, but exactly where to turn up, so nobody arrives on the right campus and then cannot find you.
- **A number.** When the tickets are gone the service shows as sold out on its own. You do not have to remember to switch anything off.
- **A list of who is coming.** You can see who has paid and is expected to collect.

It also means each location keeps its own numbers. Rozelle selling out does not affect anywhere else.



## **Setting up a meal service**



- Log in and go to your **store dashboard**.
- Open the **Events** section and choose to create a new event.
- **Name it so a student understands it** — something like *Food Hub lunch, Rozelle*. This is what they see when they pay and what appears on their confirmation.
- **Set the date and time** to the service itself, for example Wednesday 12pm to 2pm. This is the collection window, not when you start cooking.
- **Set the location to exactly where they collect** — the building, the room, the door. Not just the campus. Something like *Student Lounge and Kitchenette, Building 103, Rozelle Campus* is right; *Rozelle* on its own is not. Someone who has never been should be able to walk to it. Add a map link if there is one.
- **Tick two categories: your campus, and Food Hub.** The campus category is what tells the site which campus this location belongs to, and it is what makes the service appear when someone filters the events page by campus. Food Hub keeps it grouped with everything else the hub runs.

> **These two do different jobs.** The location says *where to stand*. The campus category says *which campus that is*, for filtering. You need both — a precise location with no campus category will not show up in the campus filter, and a campus category with a vague location leaves people wandering.

- If the service runs every week, turn on **recurring** and set it to repeat weekly. That saves rebuilding it each Wednesday.
- Add a **ticket**. Name it *Meal*, set the price to the current meal price, and set the **quantity to the number of meals you will have**.
- Publish.

You can publish straight away. Food Hub events and products do not need anyone else to approve them.



## **How the numbers work**



**The ticket quantity is your stock.** Every meal bought takes one off. When it reaches zero the service shows as sold out and nobody else can buy.

If you end up with more meals than expected, edit the event and raise the ticket quantity. If you have fewer, lower it.

> **Careful:** lowering the quantity below the number already sold will not cancel anyone. Check what has sold before you reduce it.



## **Seeing who has paid**



Each event has an **attendee list** showing who has bought a meal. That is your collection list.

Orders also appear as normal orders in the store dashboard, and an email goes out when someone buys.



## **Changing the price**



The meal price sits on the ticket, so edit the event and change the ticket price.

This only affects meals bought from that point on. Anyone who already paid keeps what they paid. If the service is recurring, check whether the change applies to future dates as well as the next one.



## **Each week**



If the service is recurring you should not need to do anything. Check the ticket quantity matches how many meals you actually have, and adjust it if not.

If it is not recurring, create the next one the same way.



## **If something is not right**



- **You cannot see an Events section** in your store dashboard — your access may not be set up yet. Ask a site administrator.
- **A student says it shows as sold out but you have meals** — the ticket quantity has run out. Edit the event and raise it.
- **Someone paid but is not on the attendee list** — check the orders in the store dashboard first, then raise it with the website team.
- **Students cannot find the service when filtering the events page by campus** — the campus category was probably not ticked. Edit the event and add it.



## **Where the money goes**



Meal payments go through the same system as everything else students buy from TUSA online. They are **not** donations, and they are recorded separately from the Food Hub donations described in [Food Hub Donations: Where the Records Are Kept](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/food-hub/selling-food-hub-meals-online/#food-hub-donations).
