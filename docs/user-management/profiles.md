# **Profiles: What Users Change, and What You Set**

This guide is for TUSA staff. It clears up a common question: when someone updates their profile, does it need approving? **No.** Nothing about profiles waits for approval anymore. This explains what a member can change themselves (which just happens), and what the content admin looks after (the public-facing role side).

> **The short version:** a member updates their own basic details and it saves straight away — there’s nothing in a queue for you. What *you* look after is the public role identity: who appears on the Reps, Board, Equity and Team pages, with their title and bio. That’s set by a content admin, from information you get from the person, when they take up the role.

---



## **What a member can change themselves**



A member can update their own everyday details from their profile, and the change applies immediately. They can change:

- Their **name** and **nickname**
- Their **pronouns**
- Their **preferred contact email** (this is not their login email — see below)
- Their own **personal bio**
- Their **relationship to the University** — whether they’re a current student, a past student, or have never been a student
- Whether they’re **under 18**

When they change their relationship to the University, their membership updates to match on its own — for example, a current student who becomes a past student is moved to past-student membership automatically. Nobody has to approve or action it.

**There is no approval step and nothing lands in anyone’s inbox.** It’s their own basic information, so they’re trusted to keep it up to date.

A member **cannot** give themselves a role like Board, Rep, staff, or committee. Those aren’t on the form at all — they’re set by TUSA (see below).

**Login email and password** aren’t part of this — a member changes those on their own **Account** settings, not their profile. If someone asks to change the email they log in with, point them there.

**Club presidents and committee** don’t update their profile this way. Their profile is tied to their club role, so they go through the club affiliation process instead. If one asks to change their details, they contact `clubs@tusa.utas.edu.au`.

## **What the content admin looks after**



The **public-facing role side** is the content admin’s job. This is the identity people see on the website:

- Who appears on the **Elected Representatives**, **Board of Management**, **Equity Committee**, **TUSA Team**, **Student Advocates** and **Experience Leaders** pages
- Each person’s **role title** on their card
- Their **public bio** and **photo**

A member can’t set any of this themselves — it’s deliberately kept in TUSA’s hands so the public pages stay accurate and consistent.



### **You gather the content, then set it**



When someone takes up a role, **you get their details from them** — their role title, a short bio, and a photo — and you set them up on the right page. The trigger is the moment they take up the role: a new representative after an election, a new board member after an appointment, an EOI outcome, a new staff member.

The step-by-step (which page, which fields, ordering) is in **[Managing Users in Grids](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/user-management/profiles-what-users-change-and-what-you-set/#managing-user-grids)** — that’s the guide for putting someone on a public page and keeping their card right.



### **Check before you give someone a role**



Before you set someone up in a public role, make sure they actually hold it. Check with the people who own that decision:


| Role | Confirm with |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| TUSA staff member | The staff list / HR onboarding |
| Board of Management | The Board Secretary |
| Elected Representative | The elections result |
| Equity Committee | The Equity Committee Chair |
| Experience Leader (TEL) | The L&D coordinator |
| Student Advocate | The Advocates lead |


If you’re not sure, ask the relevant team lead first. Don’t put someone in a public role on a hunch.

When a role ends (a term finishes, someone leaves), take them off the page the same way — see [Managing Users in Grids](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/user-management/profiles-what-users-change-and-what-you-set/#managing-user-grids).

## **Food Hub volunteers**



Food Hub volunteers aren’t set up here at all. A Food Hub group organiser adds them to the **Food Hub group**, and that sets them up to be rostered. ## **Common questions**



**“A member updated their profile — do I need to approve it?”**  
No. It’s already applied. There’s nothing for you to do.

**“Someone says they should be on the Board / Reps page but they’re not.”**  
That’s set by a content admin, not by the member. Confirm they actually hold the role (see the table above), then add them — see [Managing Users in Grids](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/user-management/profiles-what-users-change-and-what-you-set/#managing-user-grids).

**“A member tried to make themselves a Rep / staff and it didn’t work.”**  
It can’t — the form doesn’t let anyone give themselves a role. Point them at the right channel (for example, board appointments come through the Board Secretary). Once it’s confirmed, you set it up.

**“Someone’s card on a public page looks bare or wrong.”**  
That’s the content admin’s to fix — get their title, bio and photo from them and update their card. See [Managing Users in Grids](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/user-management/profiles-what-users-change-and-what-you-set/#managing-user-grids).

**“A member left a role but still shows on the page.”**  
Updating their own profile won’t remove them from a public page — that’s on TUSA. Take them off when their role ends — see [Managing Users in Grids](https://tusa-dev.its.utas.edu.au/docs/tusa-operations-guide/user-management/profiles-what-users-change-and-what-you-set/#managing-user-grids).

**“A member is still a student but their profile says past student.”**  
Have them update their profile and choose **current student** — their membership corrects itself. There’s nothing for you to approve.
